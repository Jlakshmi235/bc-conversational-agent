# What the estimate does not tell the user

## Theory Tags
- PMT: response costs / coping appraisal; self-efficacy
- FTT: gist of model limitations and uncertainty
- MI: provide neutrally, normalize uncertainty, explain that another model
  may be appropriate only after individualized clinical assessment.

Note: perceived barriers is tagged under PMT only, per the precedence rule
in 00_scope-and-safety.md.

## FTT use
- Preserve relevant verbatim details about included and not-fully-
  represented factors when the user asks for them.
- Also provide a brief gist: the Gail/BCRAT estimate is based on a defined
  set of factors and does not capture every factor that may matter
  clinically.
- Do not infer whether an omitted factor would increase or decrease the
  user's estimate.
- When details become overwhelming, return to the bottom-line limitation
  rather than adding unsupported explanation.

## PMT use
- Response costs: reduce uncertainty created by model limitations by
  clearly distinguishing what the Gail/BCRAT model includes from what it
  does not fully represent.
- Self-efficacy: reinforce that the user can bring relevant personal or
  family-history information and the result to a qualified healthcare
  professional.
- Do not use model limitations to increase perceived threat or speculate
  that omitted factors would raise or lower the user's risk.

## Tone Guidance
Neutral context.

## Included factors
The Gail/BCRAT model uses:
- Age
- Age at first menstrual period
- Age at first live birth
- Benign breast biopsy history
- Atypical hyperplasia
- Number of first-degree relatives with breast cancer
- Race or ethnicity

## Factors not fully represented
- second- and third-degree relatives, including paternal-side relatives
  such as aunts and grandmothers,
- relatives' ages at diagnosis,
- whether a relative had cancer in both breasts,
- breast density,
- known pathogenic genetic variants such as BRCA1 or BRCA2,
- the model's lifetime-risk estimate is less validated than its 5-year
  estimate; models such as Tyrer-Cuzick are more commonly used specifically
  for lifetime-risk-based assessment,
- every personal, hormonal, environmental, and imaging-related factor.

The model is not a diagnostic test. It may not be appropriate for:
- People with prior invasive breast cancer, DCIS, or LCIS.
- Certain hereditary cancer syndromes.
- Histories of chest radiation.

## Closing message
Different models are designed for different clinical situations. Choosing
another model requires individualized clinical assessment. Consult a
healthcare provider.
