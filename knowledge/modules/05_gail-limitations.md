# What the estimate does not tell the user

## Theory Tags
- PMT: response costs / coping appraisal; self-efficacy
- FTT: gist of model limitations and uncertainty
- MI: provide neutrally, normalize uncertainty, explain that another model
  may be appropriate only after individualized clinical assessment.

Note: perceived barriers is tagged under PMT only, per the precedence rule
in 00_scope-and-safety.md.

## Spoken delivery format
No markdown. Flowing sentences suitable for speech.

## Deliver this in turns — do not dump it all at once
This is the most detail-heavy module in the knowledge base. Never read the
full included/excluded factor list in one turn. Structure across multiple
exchanges:
1. First turn: one short sentence summarizing that the calculator uses a
   defined set of personal factors and doesn't capture everything, then
   ask whether they'd like to hear what it does include, what it leaves
   out, or both — let them choose.
2. Only once they choose: give that one category (included factors, or
   excluded factors) in a few natural sentences — not the full bulleted
   list read aloud, a plain-language summary of it.
3. If they want the other category too, give it on the next turn, not
   appended to the same turn.
4. Close with the one-sentence gist below only once, at the end of this
   sub-conversation, not repeated after every turn.

## FTT use
- Preserve relevant verbatim details about included and not-fully-
  represented factors when the user asks for them, delivered per the
  turn-based structure above.
- Provide a brief gist once, at the close of this topic: the Gail/BCRAT
  estimate is based on a defined set of factors and does not capture
  every factor that may matter clinically.
- Do not infer whether an omitted factor would increase or decrease the
  user's estimate.
- When details become overwhelming, return to the bottom-line limitation
  rather than adding unsupported explanation.

## PMT use
- Response costs: reduce uncertainty created by model limitations by
  clearly distinguishing what the Gail/BCRAT model includes from what it
  does not fully represent — offered a piece at a time, not all at once,
  to keep each turn manageable.
- Self-efficacy: reinforce that the user can bring relevant personal or
  family-history information and the result to a qualified healthcare
  professional.
- Do not use model limitations to increase perceived threat or speculate
  that omitted factors would raise or lower the user's risk.

## Tone Guidance
Neutral context.

## Included factors (for use only when the user asks for this category)
The Gail/BCRAT model uses: age; age at first menstrual period; age at
first live birth; benign breast biopsy history; atypical hyperplasia;
number of first-degree relatives with breast cancer; and race or
ethnicity. Summarize in plain sentences, not a read-aloud list.

## Factors not fully represented (for use only when the user asks for this category)
Not fully captured: relatives beyond first-degree, including paternal-side
relatives such as aunts and grandmothers; relatives' ages at diagnosis;
whether a relative had cancer in both breasts; breast density; known
pathogenic genetic variants such as BRCA1 or BRCA2; and the model's
lifetime-risk estimate is less validated than its 5-year estimate — models
such as Tyrer-Cuzick are more commonly used specifically for lifetime-risk
assessment. Summarize in plain sentences, not a read-aloud list.

The model is not a diagnostic test. It may not be appropriate for people
with prior invasive breast cancer, DCIS, or LCIS; certain hereditary
cancer syndromes; or histories of chest radiation. Mention this only if
relevant to what the user asked.

## Closing message (state once, at the end of this topic)
Different models are designed for different clinical situations. Choosing
another model requires individualized clinical assessment — a healthcare
professional can help determine whether another approach is appropriate.

## Closing Behavior
After the closing message, offer one next topic (healthy steps or
preparing for a clinician discussion) rather than continuing to add detail.
