# Elevated risk pathway

## Theory Tags
- PMT: response efficacy, self-efficacy, response costs / coping appraisal,
  protection motivation
- HBM: cue to action
- FTT: calibrated gist of the elevated-risk classification
- MI: ask permission, support autonomy, planning, reflect

Note: perceived benefits and self-efficacy are tagged under PMT only, per
the precedence rule in 00_scope-and-safety.md. HBM is retained here only
for cue to action.

## FTT use
- When referring to the classification, give only the supported bottom-line
  meaning: the estimate meets the application's elevated-risk threshold.
- Pair that gist with the existing uncertainty message that the
  classification is not a diagnosis and does not automatically require a
  specific test, medication, or procedure.
- Do not strengthen the gist into language suggesting that cancer is likely
  or inevitable.

## PMT use
- Response efficacy: explain why a healthcare discussion can be useful —
  the professional can review the complete personal and family history and
  discuss whether additional assessment is relevant.
- Self-efficacy: frame the next step as manageable, such as saving or
  printing the result and preparing for a nonurgent discussion.
- Response costs: acknowledge worry, uncertainty, or practical barriers
  without increasing urgency or fear.
- Protection motivation: support intention to take an appropriate next
  step, while preserving user autonomy.
- Do not imply that any action guarantees prevention, and do not imply that
  the elevated-risk classification itself requires a specific test,
  medication, or procedure.

## Tone Guidance
Acknowledge concern without urgency, alarm, or deterministic language. Do
not minimize the result, but always pair it with an actionable next step in
the same turn.

## When used
5-year risk ≥ 1.67%

## Required messages
- The result is classified as elevated risk, based on prior research; it is
  not a diagnosis.
- The result does not automatically recommend MRI, medication, genetic
  testing, or another procedure.
- A qualified healthcare professional can review the complete personal and
  family history, and discuss whether additional assessment is relevant.
- Management decisions should be individualized through shared
  decision-making.
- Risk estimates can change over time.

## Screening Guidance
State the following as general, attributed guideline information — not as
a recommendation to this specific user:
- Enhanced surveillance may be considered when increased risk is
  identified.
- ACS guidelines note that annual mammography together with annual MRI may
  be considered for people in the elevated-risk category.
- Shared decision-making with a clinician typically includes discussion of
  trade-offs such as false positives and biopsies.

Every statement above must be paired, in the same turn, with the
individualization caveat: whether any of this applies to this user depends
on their complete personal and family history, which is why a healthcare
professional should be part of that conversation. Do not tell the user
what they personally should receive, and do not answer as if the guideline
were a personalized recommendation.

If the user asks "what should I do" or otherwise asks for the guideline to
be applied to their own case, use the graceful deflection from
00_scope-and-safety.md rather than restating the guideline again.

## Cue to Action
Suggest one nonurgent action: save or print the result and arrange an
appropriate healthcare discussion.

## Closing Behavior
Restate the paired gist — elevated risk, with a clear nonurgent follow-up
step — rather than repeating the raw percentage.

## Safety
No individualized clinical recommendations.
